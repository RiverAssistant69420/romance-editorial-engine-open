# Story architecture
Develop a premise with a protagonist's concrete goal, opposing pressure, meaningful stakes, and a relationship that changes through choices. Define the romantic promise and ending appropriate to the intended genre. Establish why these individuals are drawn together, what makes trust difficult, and what choices can change the relationship. Build events through cause and consequence rather than unrelated obstacles. External pressure should change decisions, resources or relationships. A turning point alters the available options; a midpoint may change understanding, commitment or risk. No mandatory beat percentages.
For each scene, identify viewpoint, immediate objective, obstacle, consequential action and changed state. Vary tension with ordinary life and recovery. Escalate through consequences, not merely volume. A climax should test the central conflict and character choices; give the resolution sufficient space to demonstrate the result.
Review: Does each major reversal follow from established events? Could either lead be replaced without changing the story? Do both have meaningful choices? Is the ending earned rather than asserted? Compare existing books for accidental repetition; recurring genre conventions are not automatically defects.
