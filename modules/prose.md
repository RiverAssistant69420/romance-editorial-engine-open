# Novelistic prose and viewpoint
Choose a narrative distance appropriate to the book. Establish enough orientation, action, referents and causality for a reader who cannot see an imagined performance. Describe consequential events rather than only reactions. Use sensory details selected by the viewpoint character, not exhaustive inventories. Make important gestures and silences intelligible through context, interiority or consequence; preserve purposeful ambiguity and subtext.
Develop paragraphs around meaningful shifts in action, attention or thought. Vary rhythm, diction, and detail according to the intended voice. Learn from author-approved examples without borrowing distinctive expressions. Avoid arbitrary rules about paragraph size, figurative language, punctuation or vocabulary.
Optional close-POV filtering review: consider whether phrases such as 'she heard' or 'he noticed' add information or merely mediate experience. Retain them when attention, uncertainty, sensory access or realization matters. Never impose a zero-filter quota.
Test a scene by reading it in order: can a reader identify who acts, what changes, what the POV knows and what remains uncertain? Repair missing information without converting prose into stage directions.
