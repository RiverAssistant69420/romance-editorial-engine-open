# Character psychology and emotion
Give each character independent goals, values, relationships, blind spots, practical habits and a plausible history. Differentiate their attention, interpretation and decision-making rather than assigning a handful of quirks. Establish what each person knows, suspects, fears and wants at each turning point.
Emotions affect perception, behavior, speech, memory and choices; characters may respond differently to equivalent events. Avoid prescribing a universal trauma response. Let conflicting impulses coexist. Ground extreme reactions in stakes and individual history, and let consequences persist across scenes. Neither restraint nor overt expression is inherently superior.
Review: What has changed? Why does this person care? What action does the emotion motivate or inhibit? Is their response proportionate given their own beliefs? Does the scene preserve agency and uncertainty? A character can misinterpret evidence; the narrative should distinguish that inference from established fact.
