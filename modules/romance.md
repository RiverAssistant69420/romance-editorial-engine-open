# Romantic development and non-explicit intimacy
Build attraction through individual attention, shared activity, competing goals, earned trust and choices. Distinguish longing, tenderness, humor, uncertainty, grief and reconciliation. Give both adults independent intentions and meaningful agency. Prior affection, marriage and silence do not establish consent. Affection is not a substitute for accountability.
For non-explicit kisses, embraces and affectionate scenes, establish physical positions and relevant objects, maintain plausible continuity, and allow action and thought to coexist. Let responses reflect the particular relationship. Avoid a fixed escalation formula or repeated stock sensations. Ordinary affection may coexist with work, conversation or family life. Use appropriate non-explicit transitions when needed; attend to emotional consequences afterward.
Review: Is the interaction welcome and intelligible? Does the behavior belong to these people? Does the scene change or reveal something? Are unresolved conflicts preserved? Respect author preferences and applicable content limits.
